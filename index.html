How a patient reaches a service

The patient does not choose from a blank page. Their perceived need is filtered through the options the system makes visible, available and practical.

1. Need arises

Symptoms, deterioration, injury, distress, concern or advice from another person.

2. Patient judges the options

Clinical need may differ from perceived need. The choice is shaped by both perception and access.

Perceived acuity Appointment availability Opening hours Distance Expected wait Previous experience Trust Knowledge Transport

3. A service is chosen or reached

GP

Continuity, familiarity and perceived suitability, constrained by timely access.

NHS 111

Used when the patient is uncertain, cannot access another service or wants triage.

UTC

Influenced by proximity, awareness, opening hours and confidence it can resolve the problem.

A&E

Influenced by acuity, 24-hour access, perceived capability, distance and expected waits.

What if usable urgent GP appointments increased?

This first scenario tests a deliberately narrow relationship. It distinguishes minor A&E activity, total A&E activity and waiting-time performance rather than treating them as the same outcome.

Prototype baseline: 100,000 urgent GP appointments, 10,000 minor A&E attendances and 50,000 total A&E attendances per month. These are illustrative, not local data.
