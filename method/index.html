How the scores work

An adapted GRADE approach for health-services evidence.

Evidence statements How scores work Patient pathway model

Evidence score: how confident we are that the reported relationship is reliable.

Association: how strong the observed relationship appears to be.

This adapts GRADE for routine data, natural experiments and other health-services research.

1. Start from the strongest study design

4/4

High

Randomised evidence or several strong causal evaluations with consistent results.

3/4

Moderate

Controlled quasi-experimental evidence, such as difference-in-differences or interrupted time series.

2/4

Low

Adjusted observational, panel, ecological or analytical cross-sectional evidence.

1/4

Very low

Uncontrolled before-and-after studies, small surveys, descriptive studies or expert opinion.

2. Check six domains

Risk of bias

Could confounding, reverse causation, selection or recall bias explain the result?

Consistency

Do independent studies using different methods agree?

Precision

Can the estimate distinguish a useful effect from little or no effect?

Directness

Does it measure the actual NHS population, intervention and outcome?

Publication bias

Could published studies over-represent positive results?

Other factors

Large effects, dose-response or agreement across methods may increase confidence.

3. Reach the final score

Serious concerns lower the score. Convincing additional evidence may raise it. A systematic review does not score highly when the studies inside it are weak.

Framework

GRADE · ROBINS-I · GRADE upgrades

Status: initial rapid evidence review, not a completed systematic review.
